Translation

Worked example (first column): {PT 嚥} is {CM 翁} {CH ⿰口𠸜} {PT 反}: by the method [they are] combined into the character 嚥. The others follow this; the fanqie method is likewise.

Five further characters follow, each with a Nôm annotation (second column).

[Draft translation, to be checked.]

Notes

Transcribed by Claude as in E07; notation as in the evidence README.

Findings

  • The combined character at the top of the first column is, according to the proposer, the unmarked Cán of ONG on the left and the Chi CHONG-MARK-5 on the right, read yên (嚥). It has no tone mark. The marked Cán of ONG in the formula carries a tone mark (E34); the Chi CHONG-MARK-5 in the formula carries a semicircle at the lower left. A Cán on the left and a Chi of another base on the right: the phản thiết arrangement (E06).
  • The formula has the same form as the worked example in E07, with the closing formula 他倣此切法亦然.

Open

  • Placement: the passage has no heading and does not fit the Hợp Thư heading, because it shows a Cán on the left and a Chi on the right. The pages are not in the wrong order. The last column of p. 6 ends at about 40 per cent of the page height, which marks the end of the section before (E07); a double page may be missing (proposer's observation). Whether the passage belongs to the section 反切諧音法 (E05) or to another unit is not established.
  • The five characters of the second column, each the unmarked Cán of ONG on the left (transcription of the proposer, S1-p7-text): ONG J GIONG-MARK-5 (⿰口淵), ONG J XONG-MARK-5 (焉; both sources show a vertical line between the two sides, possibly an error), ONG J KHONG-MARK-5 (淵), ONG J SONG-MARK-3 (卬), ONG J SONG-MARK-5 (喧), the last four with the neutral tone mark.
  • The Chi in the formula is annotated ⿰口𠸜 (no encoded character found); 𲕠 (⿰口昭) was an earlier reading.
