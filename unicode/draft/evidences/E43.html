Transcription

茲模索音畫創爲一樣書。以便私記已也。豈敢云通倮蠱之聲音乎哉。

Translation

So, groping for sound strokes, I created a kind of writing, only to make my private notes easier. How would I dare to say that it conveys the sounds of human creatures? [Draft translation, to be checked; 倮蠱 is read as 倮蟲, see Notes]

Notes

Transcription of the proposer (transcription/S1-pp1-5.md). The scan has 蠱 (gǔ, 'poison', 'worm infestation'), after 倮 ('naked'). The translation reads it as 倮蟲, 'the naked creatures', the classical term for human beings among the creatures (the Da Dai Liji counts 360 kinds of 倮蟲 with the sage at their head); 蠱 and 蟲 share the component 虫 and are easily confused in copying. The reading fits the sense of the sentence, a modest disclaimer, but it is not certain. The first part of the sentence is clear: the script serves the author's private notes and he makes no claim beyond that. Nguyễn Quang Hồng (1986) reports the same (E42); this entry rests on the manuscript alone.
