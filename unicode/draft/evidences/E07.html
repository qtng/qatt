Transcription

合書左從枝体右從幹体畧法

{QT 螉}乃{QT 喊}{CM 翁}{PT 反}法合螉字也他倣此切法亦如之

{QT 癰} {QT 螉} {QT 嗡} {QT ⿰口癰}

Translation

Heading: Method of the Hợp Thư (合書), 畧法 [not translated]: the left follows the Chi body (枝体), the right follows the Cán body (幹体).

Worked example (seventh column): {QT 螉} is {QT 喊} {CM 翁} {PT 反}: by the method [they are] combined into the character 螉. The others follow this; the fanqie method is likewise.

Four further characters follow, each with a Nôm annotation (eighth column).

[Draft translation, to be checked. The placeholders stand for QATT characters, the character after the class is the small Nôm annotation beside it. The role of 乃 and of the QATT character annotated 反 in the formula is not established.]

Notes

Transcribed by Claude from the images of both sources (Source 2 is clearer and was read first, Source 1 compared); the readings of the small annotations are not all certain, see Open. Notation as in the evidence README; {QT …} is a QATT character whose class (marked or unmarked Cán, Chi, pair) has not been determined, {CM 翁} the marked Cán, identified by the proposer (E34).

Findings

  • The section on the Hợp Thư runs from p. 6, sixth column from the right (heading), to the last column of p. 6. It consists of one worked example in running text (seventh column) and a list of four characters with Nôm annotations and no text (eighth column).
  • The worked example has the form: a QATT character with its annotation, 乃, two further QATT characters, the character annotated 反, 法合, the annotation of the first character written out as a Nôm character, 字也, and the closing formula 他倣此切法亦如之.
  • Layout evidence (proposer's observation, measured on the images): the last column of p. 6, the list of four characters, ends at about 40 per cent of the page height (ink down to about 590 of 1,480 pixels in both sources), while the other columns of the page run to the bottom edge. In the manuscript a column that stops half-way marks the end of a text unit; the only other such ending is the last column of the whole text on p. 8. The section therefore ends here. The passage on p. 7, first and second column, is not part of it (E39).
  • One of the two occurrences of the marked Cán outside the listing of the 22 Cán is in the worked example (E34): seventh column, fourth character.
  • The Nôm character that closes the formula (螉) is the same as the annotation beside the first QATT character of the formula.
  • The proposer reads the formula as follows (not yet checked against the originals): the Cán is written in its marked form, the form of the listing, as the name of the base; in the combined character explained by the formula it stands, without marking stroke, as the unmarked Cán. The column that follows shows the same character with the marking position of the Chi, on the left, run through the positions, while the Cán, on the right, has no marking stroke. This would make the example a Hợp Thư with the Chi on the left and the Cán on the right, as the heading says, and the marked Cán in the formula a name for the unmarked Cán of the combined character.

Open

  • Annotations that are not certain: 癰 and ⿰口癰 (eighth column, first and fourth); 螉 is 虫 with 翁 and is read ong ('bee') in Vietnamese. To be checked against the originals.
  • Class and tone marks of the QATT characters are not transcribed, except for the occurrence of the marked Cán.
  • Whether the example shows that in a Hợp Thư the Chi gives the initial and the Cán the rhyme (E08) is not concluded here; the readings of the annotations would have to be examined.
