Introduction

Quốc Âm Tân Tự - abbreviated QATT - is a semisyllabic writing system that first appeared in historical records between 1841 and 1847. The system consists of 22 base symbols that can be combined with to form syllables. Words are built by combining two components to form a character.

Spelling

The left part contributes the onset, the right part contributes the rhyme. In this example the left part is the onset x and the right part is the rhyme nga. Together it is read xa. There are 110 rhyme parts that can be used to write the Vietnamese language.

Tham Thư - Clustered spelling

Rhymes that end in a coda (e.g. sao, sen, sang) can be written in an alternative way called Tham Thư. This method places the coda as a separate component in the top half of the character. Tham Thư is also the basis for cursive writing and calligraphy. In calligraphy the top part is often reduced to a single stroke or dot and it might be placed at the side or bottom.

Level 1 Symbols

The listed 22 consonants and 25 rhyme symbols are sufficient for calligraphy and normal writing using Tham Thư.

Important

  • ng/nh coda is both written with ng.
  • the Anh rhyme is written with lơ + ng instead of nga + ng.
  • The final u in nâu, tuâu, thau, nhoau is silent when a coda is added. For example thay is written tha(u)+i where the u is silent.
