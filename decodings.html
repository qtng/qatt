Please note that the shown decodings can only be aproximations. Quốc Âm Tân Tự is not fully decipherable because the original rhyme table is written in Chữ Nôm, which often has ambiguous pronunciations. Furthermore a lot of the annotation characters don't exist in any dictionary, so the intended pronunciation is completely unknown. This leads to the rhyme table being underdetermined, not allowing for full recovery without asserting additional hypotheses. Another aspect leading to inconsistencies is that the rhyme table assigns at least six types of rhymes (differentiated by coda) to only five symbol groups denoted by 5 markings, shown above as 5 rows. This led to the 6th rhyme type being scattered all over the place where phonotactical restrictions left an empty slot. A small number of these fill-in rhymes have unclear or competing Chữ Nôm pronunciations and can only be deciphered by applying the bijection hypotheses and guesswork. These are mostly the 8 rhymes in the bottom right corner. The position in the last columns and the last rows suggest that those are the rhymes the author assigned last.

The following is a list of hypotheses that ate used in the in the decodings shown on this page.

Hypotheses

Bijection hypothesis: Even though some of the original annotations have he same rhyme in modern Hán Nôm, all known decodings assume that each rhyme occurs only once. Used by: omniglot.org, qatt.org

Pair hypotheses: The rounded variant of a rhyme and its unrounded rhyme occur pairwise. This hypotheses often includes the assumption that the khẩu radical (口) often denotes the roundednes of a rhyme instead of being part of the Chữ Nôm character itself. Used by: qatt.org, to a lesser extent by omniglot.com

Symmetry hypotheses: Each unrounded rhyme is the mirror image of its unrounded rhyme. This is a stricter variant of the pair hypotheses. This leads to a shift of a number of rhymes that would otherwise violate this condition. Used by: qatt.org

Nucleus hypothesis: The 22 base symbols represent the 22 nucleus rhymes of the Vietnamese language. Used by: qatt.org

Minority language hypotheses: The superfluous nh/ng rhymes represent certain rhymes of minority languages ending in ng. This hypotheses excludes the vận thư hypotheses. Used by: omniglot.org

Vận thư hypotheses: The 6 superfluous rhymes ending in nh/ng reflect the fact that old rhyme dictionaries distinguish between rhymes that modern Quốc Ngữ write with nh coda. E.g. while Quốc Ngữ knows only one anh rhyme, old rhyme dictionaries distinguish two separate anh rhymes, which have merged in modern Vietnamese. The reasoning is that the original document shows strong knowledge in ancient rhyme tables. This hypotheses excludes the minority language hypotheses. Used by: qatt.org

Decodings

The omniglot.com decoding
Bijection hypotheses Pair hypotheses Minority language hypotheses
This decoding optimizes best fit with modern Quốc Ngữ pronunciation. 59% of the rhymes have a pronunciation that match at least one of the modern pronunciations of the original Chữ Nôm annotation. The rest is more or less similar but not exact. This decoding uses the minority language hypotheses and therefore assigns rhymes like ooeng and uưng. It also assigns the rhymes ung, ong and ông even though the inventor of Quốc Âm Tân Tự explicitly described a separate method to write these particular rhymes called Tham Thư.

The qatt.org decoding
Bijection hypotheses Symmetry hypotheses Nucleus hypotheses Vận thư hypotheses
This decoding trades exact pronunciation matches for homogenity. It maintains an overall similary with modern pronunciation instead of seeking for exact matches. 38% of the rhymes exactly match modern pronunciation. The rest is more or less similar but not exact. This encoding uses the nucleus hypothesis and the vận thư hypotheses to handle inconsistencies found in Chữ Nôm annotations of the original rhyme table.
