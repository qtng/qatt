What the style guide is

The source of Quốc Âm Tân Tự gives the rhymes of its 110 Chi only through Nôm annotations, and not all of them can be read with certainty. A decoding is an attempt to read that rhyme table. The decoding of qatt.org is one of them.

The style guide is not a decoding. It is a definition: the spelling that qatt.org uses to write Vietnamese today, as a newspaper has a style guide of its own. It starts from the decoding qatt.org and changes it where the decoding leaves a rhyme of Vietnamese without a place or puts it far from its vowel. Its aim is a table that can be learnt: every row holds the rhymes of one vowel.

The rules

  1. The basis is the decoding qatt.org. Every rhyme that is not named below keeps its place, including the exchange of the rows CHONG and XONG, GIONG and KHONG.
  2. The unmarked Cán writes the rhymes with the coda u or o. In a pair, an unmarked Cán on the right is the rhyme of the row of its vowel: ao on NGONG, eo on GONG, âu on NONG, iu on SONG and so on. The position decides: on the left an unmarked Cán is the initial, on the right the rhyme. Example: cao.
  3. ôm, um, om and uôm stand on MONG-6, BONG-6, VONG-6 and PHONG-6, in the rows of ô, u, o and ua.
  4. ư and ươ stand on TRONG-2 and DONG-2, the first Chi of their rows.
  5. uêu, which has no place in the decoding, is the unmarked Cán of GIONG.
  6. The places that are freed stay empty. They are obsolete for Vietnamese, not given to other rhymes, so that a text after the decoding is never read wrongly as a different word.

The rhyme table

Choosing a rhyme table

The editor, the wiki and the Notes app write after the style guide unless another table is chosen in their menu; a decoding can be chosen there as well. Spell it! and the course use the style guide only. Text that was written after a decoding is converted when its table is changed.
