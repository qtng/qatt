The script

Quốc Âm Tân Tự (國音新字) means “new script for the sounds of the national language”. It was created in the 1840s, under Emperor Thiệu Trị. Only one text survives, in two manuscripts in Hanoi.

The script has 22 basic shapes of three strokes each. A small extra stroke in one of six places gives six more forms. Two shapes side by side make one syllable: the left one is its beginning, the right one its ending. Small circles and half circles in the corners mark the eight tones.

Unicode proposal

Unicode lets every script be shown on every device. QATT is not part of it yet. A draft proposal adds 164 characters: the shapes as building blocks and a joining character that puts them together.

Decodings

The only source explains the script with a table in Chữ Nôm. Many of its hints are unclear or lost, so the sounds of the symbols can only be reconstructed with some assumptions. The decodings show the result and compare the reading of this site with the one on Omniglot.
